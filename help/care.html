Care & Keeping

The whole idea behind Gart's is to buy the right thing once and then actually keep it. A little care goes a long way. Here's how to look after each kind of piece.

Stoneware planters & vases

Stoneware is tougher than it looks, but ceramic's one true enemy is a sudden change in temperature. A few habits keep yours whole for years:

  • Wash gently, by hand. Warm water, a soft sponge, mild soap. Skip the dishwasher — the heat swings and jostling are how chips start.
  • No thermal shocks. Don't pour boiling water into a cold pot or set a warm planter on freezing tile. Let things come to room temperature.
  • Mind the drainage hole. Rinse it out now and then so it doesn't clog with soil and salt. Good drainage is what keeps both your plant and your pot happy.
  • Watch for white residue. Chalky mineral buildup on the outside is just hard-water salts. A wipe with a cloth dampened in equal-parts water and white vinegar lifts it; rinse after.
  • Lift, don't drag. Sliding a full planter across a counter is how rims crack. Two hands, lift and place.
  • Bring pots in before a freeze. Water trapped in ceramic can expand and crack it in a hard frost. If a planter lives outside, empty and store it over winter.

The Balcony Tool Set — steel & ash

The trowel, transplanter, and snips are forged steel with ash-wood handles. Treated well, they'll outlast several gardens.

  • Clean after each use. Knock off the dirt, wipe the blades dry. Soil left on steel holds moisture, and moisture is what starts rust.
  • Dry fully before storing. A quick towel-off is enough. Never put them away damp.
  • Oil the ash handles. A few times a season, rub a little linseed or a food-safe wood oil into the ash and let it soak in. It keeps the wood from drying out, splitting, or splintering in your hand.
  • Oil the steel too. A thin film of oil on the blades before winter storage wards off rust. A drop of camellia or mineral oil on a rag does it.
  • Sharpen the snips. When they start crushing stems instead of slicing them cleanly, it's time. A few passes with a fine sharpening stone or a carbide pruner sharpener along the existing bevel restores the edge. Sharp snips make cleaner cuts, which are healthier for your plants.
  • Store dry. A drawer or a hook out of the weather beats leaving them on a damp balcony floor.

Woven trays & small decor

  • Dust, don't drown. Woven pieces prefer a dry cloth or a soft brush. If something spills, blot it and let it air-dry fully rather than soaking it.
  • Keep out of standing water. A tray under a leaky pot will eventually warp or stain. Use a saucer, and empty it.
  • Rotate sun-facing pieces. Strong, constant direct sun can fade natural fibers over months. An occasional turn evens it out.
  • Handle limited decor kindly. Small-batch pieces aren't coming back, so give them a soft spot away from high-traffic edges and curious elbows.

When something does break

It happens. If it's a shipping injury, we replace it — see returns. If it broke on your watch after a good long life, that's the sign it was well used. Consider the second-life shelf for a replacement, and compost or recycle what you can of the old one.
