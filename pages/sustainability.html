Sustainability, honestly

We're a small shop, not a lab, so we're going to be straight with you: we're not carbon-neutral, we don't have a dozen certifications, and we're not going to drape this page in green leaves and vague promises. Here's what we actually do, and where we fall short.

The most sustainable thing we sell is longevity

The single biggest lever isn't packaging — it's making things you don't have to replace. A stoneware planter you keep for a decade, a tool set you sharpen instead of rebuy: those never become the pile of cracked plastic pots and bent trowels that fill landfills every spring. "Buy the right thing once" is a sustainability policy dressed up as a shopping philosophy. It's the part we're proudest of.

Molded pulp, no plastic

Stoneware is fragile, so it needs real protection in transit — but that doesn't have to mean plastic foam. Every order ships double-boxed with molded pulp cradles made from recycled paper fiber. The cradles are curbside-recyclable and compostable, the boxes are recycled cardboard, and there's no plastic foam, no air pillows, no plastic tape where we can avoid it. It costs us more than foam. We think it's worth it, and enough of you have told us the same.

The second-life shelf

When a piece comes back to us in good condition, throwing it away would be absurd — it's a perfectly good pot. So we clean it up and put it back out on our second-life shelf at a discount. A returned item gets a real second home instead of a landfill, and a shopper gets our stuff for less. Damaged returns that can't be re-homed get broken down for materials recycling where we can. Returns become circulation, not waste. (How returns work: returns page.)

Small batches, less waste

We keep our decor runs limited partly for the reasons on our why Gart's page — but it's also a waste story. Making a small batch that sells through beats overproducing a big run that ends up discounted into oblivion or dumped. Fewer things, made better, sold to people who actually want them.

Where we fall short

Being honest means naming the gaps. Our stoneware is fired in a kiln, and firing takes energy — there's no zero-impact ceramic. We ship physical objects, and shipping has a footprint. We don't yet offset our freight, and we're not going to claim we do. We're working on tightening these over time, and we'd rather tell you where we are than sell you a fairy tale. If sustainability is why you're here, we respect that enough to be truthful about it.
