Keep your plants alive: a beginner's honest guide

From the Journal

If you've stood over a crispy, brown, formerly-green thing wondering where it all went wrong — welcome, you're among friends. Nearly everyone who's good with plants killed a bunch first. The good news is that keeping a houseplant alive comes down to a small number of things, and once they click, it gets easy. Here's the honest version, no jargon, no guilt.

Mistake #1: buying the plant before reading the room

The most common way a plant dies is that it never had a chance — it needed bright light and got put in a dim corner, or the reverse. Before you fall for a plant, look at where it'll live. A bright, sunny window can grow almost anything, including herbs. A spot near a window but out of the direct beam suits most classic houseplants. A dim interior corner needs a tough survivor like a snake plant or a ZZ plant. Match the plant to the light you have, and you've won half the battle before you've watered once.

Mistake #2: loving it to death with water

This is the big one. Overwatering kills far more houseplants than forgetting to water. Roots need air as much as they need moisture, and soil that's constantly soggy suffocates and rots them. The fix is a simple habit: check before you pour. Push a finger an inch into the soil. If it's damp, wait. If it's dry, water thoroughly — enough that a little runs out the bottom — then leave it alone until it dries again. Most plants would rather be a touch too dry than a touch too wet.

Mistake #3: a pot that can't drain

Even perfect watering can't save a plant sitting in a pot with nowhere for the water to go. Drainage is the unsung hero of plant survival. Use a pot with a drainage hole (ours have one, plus a saucer to catch the runoff), and empty the saucer after the plant has had its drink so roots aren't left standing in water. If you love a decorative pot with no hole, keep the plant in a plain nursery pot inside it and lift it out to water at the sink. Do that one thing and you'll dodge the slow, soggy death that gets so many beginners.

The check people forget: before a new plant goes on a shelf your cat or dog can reach, look it up on a pet-safe list. A lot of popular houseplants — pothos, lilies, aloe, philodendron and more — are toxic to pets if chewed. It's a two-minute search that saves a scary vet trip. Safe, easy starters include spider plants, calatheas, and kitchen herbs like basil and mint.

A few gentle extras

  • Rotate now and then. Plants lean toward the light. A quarter-turn every couple of weeks keeps them growing evenly instead of reaching sideways.
  • Dust the leaves. A wipe with a damp cloth lets them actually catch the light. Dusty leaves are lazy leaves.
  • Ease off in winter. Most plants grow slower in the dark months and want less water. Let the soil dry a bit more between drinks.
  • Don't panic over a lost leaf. A yellow leaf here and there is normal. Plants shed. Watch the overall trend, not every single leaf.

Pick a forgiving plant to rebuild your confidence

If your plant history is a graveyard, start with something nearly unkillable — a snake plant, a pothos, or a pot of herbs on a sunny sill. A couple of easy wins does wonders for your nerve, and before long you'll be the friend other people text photos of their sad ferns to.
