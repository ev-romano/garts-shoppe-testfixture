What it is

A handwoven tray, about 16 by 11 inches, with a low raised edge and two cut-out handles. It's the small, unglamorous hero of a tidy room — the thing that turns a scatter of stuff into "a spot." Sturdy enough to actually carry a loaded coffee-and-toast breakfast, light enough to move with one hand.

Where it earns its keep

  • Entryway. Keys, sunglasses, the mail you keep meaning to open. Everything lands in one place instead of migrating across the counter.
  • Side table or ottoman. Remote, a candle, a small vase, a book — corralled into a little vignette instead of clutter.
  • Kitchen counter. Gather oils, salt, and a sponge caddy so wiping down the counter is a five-second job.
  • Bathroom or vanity. Rounds up the daily bottles so the surface reads calm.

Materials

Handwoven natural seagrass over a solid frame, with a tight, even weave that holds its shape and doesn't shed. The color is the fiber's own — a warm natural straw tone with gentle variation, because it's a real plant material and not a printed pattern. That variation is the point; no two are identical.

"Is it just decor?"

Nope — it's a container that happens to be handsome. Its whole job is to hold things and make a surface make sense. If you're the kind of person whose entryway collects a small avalanche by Thursday, this is a genuinely useful $26, not a shelf ornament.

Care

Keep it out of soaking wet conditions — natural fiber and standing water aren't friends. Dust it with a dry brush or give it a quick vacuum with a brush attachment. If something spills, blot it and let it air-dry fully. Treated this way it holds up for years of daily use. More in the help center.
