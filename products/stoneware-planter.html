Why $42 is the right price

You can absolutely find a $9 pot at a big-box store. We've bought plenty. Here's the honest math on why this one costs more and still saves you money: the cheap pot usually has no drainage (so your roots rot, or you drill it yourself), no real saucer (so you improvise, or your windowsill warps), and a thin glaze that crazes and chips within a season. You replace it. Then you replace it again.

This one is high-fired stoneware — dense, chip-resistant, freeze-tolerant enough for a covered balcony — with the drainage and saucer built in. Buy it once. Over the years you'd cycle through cheap pots, $42 is the frugal choice, not the splurge. That's the whole Gart's idea: buy the right thing once.

"Is it just decor, or will I actually use it?"

It's a working pot first. The drainage hole and glazed saucer mean you can plant directly into it and water it like a real gardener, not just drop a nursery pot inside and hope. It happens to look good — but if you never planted a thing in it and used it to hold pens, it'd still earn its keep. Function first, looks second. Always.

Durability & shipping

Stoneware is tough in use and honestly a little nervous in transit — so we don't leave it to chance. Each planter ships double-boxed with molded pulp cradles (no plastic foam), saucer packed separately in its own nest. In the rare case it arrives cracked, we replace it immediately and we don't ask for a photo the first time — see returns. Ships free in the US over $50; details on shipping. Heads up: we don't ship stoneware outside North America, because we haven't found a way to get it there intact and we won't pretend otherwise.

A note on plants, pets, and kids

We sell the pot, not the plant — so the pot itself is inert, food-safe glaze, nothing to worry about around pets or little hands. The thing to check is whatever you put in it: a number of popular houseplants (pothos, philodendron, lilies and more) are toxic to cats and dogs if chewed. Please check your specific plant against a pet-safety list before bringing it home. We'd rather flag it than have you find out the hard way.

Care & use

Rinse with water and a soft brush; skip the dishwasher and harsh scouring so the glaze stays glossy. Empty standing water from the saucer so roots don't sit wet. If you use it outdoors, bring it in before a hard freeze — stoneware is freeze-tolerant, but water trapped in soil expands and can crack any pot. More care notes live in the help center.
